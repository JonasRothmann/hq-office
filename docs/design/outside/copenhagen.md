# City mode, Copenhagen-ish

The owner asked for the city mode to look like Copenhagen. The nature mode is unchanged. The town stays original: no
real street, building, landmark, livery or logo is copied. What it borrows is the grammar of the place: what colours
sit next to each other, how high the blocks are, what is on the pavement and how the water meets the street.

## Ten things that make a street read as Copenhagen

1. **The colour changes from house to house.** Facades along one street are painted different colours, so the street
   reads as a band of colour: warm yellow ("Nyboder yellow"), orange, ochre, red, pink and soft blue and green
   beside each other. Many are protected, so the palette has lasted.
   ([Strömma: the colours of Copenhagen houses](https://stromma.com/en-dk/copenhagen/stromma-copenhagen-blog/the-colors-of-copenhagen-houses-in-every-shade),
   [VisitDenmark: most colourful streets](https://www.visitdenmark.com/denmark/destinations/copenhagen/most-colourful-streets))
2. **Mid-rise, not towers.** The residential districts are almost entirely 3 to 5 storey houses, with parks between
   them. ([Wikipedia: Frederiksberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksberg))
3. **Perimeter blocks round courtyards.** Multistorey buildings (red brick is common in Frederiksberg) form blocks
   that enclose semi-private courtyards or gardens; a street front is one continuous wall.
   ([Dezeen: Thurøhus, "a reinterpretation of the typical Frederiksberg perimeter block"](https://www.dezeen.com/?p=1011204))
4. **A straight canal with a row of bright narrow houses.** The canal-side townhouses are brightly coloured, in wood,
   brick and plaster; the sunny side is lined with cafés and bars facing the water; the quay works as a square.
   ([Wikipedia: Nyhavn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyhavn))
5. **Old wooden boats moored along the quay.** Privately owned wooden ships (schooners, a galleass, a lightvessel)
   lie along one side, museum ships along the other. ([Wikipedia: Nyhavn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyhavn))
6. **Gables to the street, some stepped.** Stepped gables are numerous in Denmark, on Dutch-style houses and old
   churches. ([Wikipedia: Stepped gable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepped_gable))
7. **Green copper roofs.** The green copper roofs "characterize Copenhagen"; copper on the waterfront weathers fast
   in the salt air. ([Archilovers: Nordea on Christiansbro, the architect's note](https://www.archilovers.com/projects/235304/nordea-on-christiansbro.html))
8. **A bike track on each side, between pavement and road, and blue across junctions.** One-way tracks in the
   direction of traffic, on a level between the road and the pavement, a stone kerb on each side, at least 2.2 m wide;
   where parking exists, the track runs inside the parked cars. At junctions the track carries on as a broad blue
   band (invented in Copenhagen in 1981). Stop lines for cars are pulled back.
   ([Wikipedia: Cycling in Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Copenhagen),
   [Cycling Solutions: road stretches](https://cyclingsolutions.info/solutions-for-road-stretches/),
   [Trafitec: blue cycle crossings](https://trafitec.dk/safety-effects-of-blue-cycle-crossings-a-before-after-study/))
9. **Bikes everywhere, cargo bikes among them, buses yellow.** About 62 % commute by bike; 560,000 bikes share
   48,000 stands, so bikes lean outside every house; box-front cargo bikes carry children and goods; one main street
   is called the busiest cycling street in the world. The city buses are yellow.
   ([Wikipedia: Cycling in Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Copenhagen),
   [Wikipedia: Nørrebro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%B8rrebro),
   [Wikipedia: A-bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-bus_(Copenhagen)))
10. **Lamps hung over the street, and cobbles.** Dome lamps hang on wires over the middle of the street, so the
    pavement has no lamp posts. Old streets and pavements are cobbled, with newer rectangular slabs laid beside the
    stones. ([Monocle: iconic urban design details](https://monocle.com/affairs/urbanism/iconic-urban-design-details/),
    [The cobblestone sidewalks of Denmark](https://steemit.com/@geke/the-cobblestone-sidewalks-of-denmark))

## What the town does with them

At Sims scale (a person is 1.35 m, a storey 2.95 m), round the office's lot:

| Cue | In the town |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Plaster in calm pastels and ochres (ochre, terracotta, mustard, sage, dusty blue, pink, cream) and some red brick; neighbours never share a colour. |
| 2, 3 | Every street front is a continuous row of houses 3 to 6 storeys tall (the sight rule keeps the ones across from the lot at 3), with a courtyard of trees inside the block. A few blocks are squares. |
| 4, 5 | A straight canal runs past one side of the lot, one street away. Across it, a row of narrow houses with their gables to the water, cafés with awnings on the quay, and moored boats (a two-masted wooden ship, small sailboats, open motor boats) bobbing. A footbridge crosses it. |
| 6 | Many roofs turn a gable to the street; the canal houses and a few corner houses have stepped gables. Roofs are dark slate or red tile. |
| 7 | A civic building with a copper-green roof and a slim copper spire, out where it cannot hide the office. |
| 8 | Every street: pavement 2.6 m, bike track 1.8 m (on a level between road and pavement, a kerb each side), a 6 m carriageway; a blue band carries each track across each junction; car stop lines stand back. |
| 9 | More cyclists than cars (up to 60 cyclists, at most 30 cars and buses), some on cargo bikes; bikes parked in rows along the pavements; a yellow bus on a route. |
| 10 | Lamps hang on wires across the street, house to house (a slim mast where there is no house); setts on the side streets and squares, granite slabs on the pavements. At night the windows and café fronts are warm, the hanging lamps throw pools on the road, and the canal carries their light. |

The calm saturation rule stays: the surroundings frame the office and never compete with it.
