# Warm Nordic studio: notes

The direction: a Copenhagen or Stockholm design studio (pale oak, birch plywood, white and warm-grey plaster, wool,
paper pendants, plants everywhere) seen through the Sims 1 camera. Japanese touches in moderation.

## Ten visual takeaways

From the references below (real Nordic offices) and `docs/research/sims1-look.md` (the Sims 1 numbers).

1. **Three woods, not one.** Real Nordic studios layer pale oak (floors, tables), birch plywood with visible dark
   edge plies (shelving, joinery) and a darker smoked or walnut accent (one table, a frame). One flat beige reads as
   a toy; three tones read as furniture.
2. **Walls are warm white plaster with one wooden feature.** Plaster walls (Liewood: "oak, plaster and steel") with
   a single slatted oak or plywood wall per room (IKEA's Malmö hub: plywood walls). Skirting boards and window
   reveals in oak tie the floor to the walls.
3. **Daylight comes from big windows.** Tall windows with thin black or oak frames and deep sills, in a rhythm along
   the back wall. In the Sims cutaway the back wall is the one you always see, so it carries the windows.
4. **Paper pendants make the human scale.** OEO's Pas Normal office hangs large paper pendants over the desks; Aspekt
   Office designs glass pendants. Every table gets a pendant, low over it, on a long cord. At night those pools of
   warm light are the picture.
5. **The long table is the heart.** Work & Co's kitchen centres on a six-metre oak table; the kitchen is sand-grey
   cabinetry. The break area needs one long communal table with mixed chairs, not a corner sofa alone.
6. **Shelves are full.** Floor-to-ceiling shelving (Natur & Kultur: poles and shelves with oak cabinets; Work & Co: an
   oak bookcase with an alcove) filled with books in mixed coloured spines, lying stacks, ceramics, boxes, a plant.
   Object density on shelves and desks is what says "lived in".
7. **Plants at three heights.** A floor tree (fiddle-leaf fig, olive) in each room, mid plants (monstera, snake
   plant) on the floor or a sideboard, trailing pothos from a high shelf, and a small plant on a desk. Pots in
   terracotta, white and charcoal.
8. **Textiles soften the floor.** Wool rugs in oatmeal and grey with a simple border or stripe under each table group;
   a boucle or wool sofa in oatmeal, cushions in rust, sage and mustard. The accents are muted earth colours, never
   saturated primaries.
9. **Sims 1: flat even light, colour from materials, chunky readable objects.** The reference lights rooms evenly
   (people are lit flat, the two wall axes differ by 15 %), no hard shadows; detail lives in textures, not geometry.
   Objects are big enough to read at the medium zoom (45 px per metre): a book shelf reads by its coloured spines,
   a person by hair colour, top colour and trouser colour.
10. **Sims 1: tall back walls, low capped front walls, small people.** Walls 2.95 m over 1.35 m people; the near
    walls drop to 0.35 m kerbs with a dark cap. Furniture stays on the tile grid. Per-room light at night: lit rooms
    glow among blue-dark ones. Outside, a lawn and garden frame the house like a doll's house on a board.

- IKEA Creative Hub, Malmö: plywood walls and coloured cushions in meeting rooms ([Minimalissimo](https://minimalissimo.com/articles/ikea-creative-hub-malmo)).
- The Sims (2000): "the house and all its objects are pre-rendered and displayed dimetrically", the Sims in 3D
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sims_(video_game))); the numbers in `docs/research/sims1-look.md`.

Every model, texture and print in the showroom is drawn in code from primitives; nothing is copied from a game, a
furniture maker or a brand, and no furniture is named after a real design.
